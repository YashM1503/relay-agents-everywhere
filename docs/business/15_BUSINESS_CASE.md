# Business Case

## End-user value
- fewer abandoned digital workflows
- less routine dependence on family/staff
- lower interface/cognitive burden
- one consistent way to get help
- clearer control around consequential actions

## Potential buyers
### Healthcare systems / clinics
Support burden around registration, portals, forms, follow-up.

### Government/public services
Accessibility barriers and call-center burden.

### Insurers / banks
Document workflows and drop-off.

### Transit / mobility
Accessible recovery when routes/entrances change.

### Senior living / community services
Fragmented digital systems.

## Initial wedge
Do not sell “universal life assistant.”
Sell:
> accessible completion of one high-friction workflow.

Examples:
- clinic registration
- benefits renewal
- transit disruption recovery
- insurance documentation

## Pilot metrics
- completion rate
- time to completion
- staff-assistance minutes
- abandonment
- user interventions
- consequential actions correctly confirmed
- user comprehension
- confidence
- false interruption rate

## Viability hypothesis
A B2B buyer cares when:
- workflow is frequent
- failure/assistance cost is measurable
- support labor is expensive
- accessibility/regulatory pressure exists
- completion has direct service/revenue value

RELAY should prove materially higher self-service completion or several minutes less staff assistance per difficult interaction.
